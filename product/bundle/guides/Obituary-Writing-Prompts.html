Obituary Writing Prompts

A gentle, step-by-step worksheet. Answer only the questions that speak to you — even half of them is plenty. Print this page and write by hand, or jot answers on your phone.

Before you begin

There is no wrong way to do this. An obituary is not a résumé — it's a love letter with a few facts in it. Aim for 200–500 words for a program (about 4–7 short paragraphs). Write the way you'd talk about them at the kitchen table.

Part 1 · The facts (2 minutes)

  • Full name (and maiden name or nickname):
  • Date & place of birth:
  • Date & place of passing:
  • Parents' names:
  • Spouse / partner and year married:
  • Schooling, service, or trade:
  • Work they did (or were proudest of):
  • Church, unit, club, or community they belonged to:

Part 2 · Who they really were (the heart of it)

  • If you close your eyes, where do you picture them? What are they doing?
  • What did they love? (Foods, songs, teams, seasons, hobbies, places)
  • What could they do better than anyone? (Fixing things? Listening? Sweet potato pie?)
  • What did they always say? A phrase everyone will recognize instantly.
  • How did they show love — words, cooking, showing up, quiet acts?
  • What did they teach you without ever calling it a lesson?
  • What will Sunday afternoons (or holidays, or phone calls) be missing now?
  • What made them laugh — and what did their laugh sound like?

Part 3 · Family they leave behind

  • Preceded in death by:
  • Survived by — children (and spouses):
  • Grandchildren / great-grandchildren (names or a count):
  • Siblings, dear friends, special caregivers:

Tip: for large families, “seven grandchildren and a host of nieces, nephews, and friends” is warm and traditional — you don't have to list everyone.

Part 4 · Put it together — fill-in template

[Full name], [age], of [town], passed away [peacefully / surrounded by family] on [date]. [He/She] was born on [birth date] in [birthplace] to [parents].


[First name] [grew up / graduated / served / married]… (one paragraph: the shape of their life — school, love, work, faith, service)


Everyone who knew [first name] knew [the thing they loved most]… (one paragraph: passions, personality, that phrase they always said)


[He/She] was preceded in death by [names], and leaves to cherish [his/her] memory [survivors].


A celebration of [first name]'s life will be held on [date, time] at [location]. [Optional: In lieu of flowers, the family kindly requests…]

Opening lines, if you're stuck

  • “[Name] loved three things above all: her Lord, her family, and her garden — usually in that order, but the garden had its days.”
  • “On [date], heaven gained a quiet man with strong hands and a soft heart.”
  • “If you ever ate at [Name]'s table, you were family. Everyone was family.”
  • “[Name] answered every call — from his country, from his church, and from anyone who needed him.”
  • “She never met a stranger, never missed a birthday, and never once lost at spades — just ask her.”

Final check

  1. Read it out loud — your ear will catch what your eye misses.
  2. Double-check every name spelling and date (the most common regrets).
  3. Ask one other family member to read it before printing.
  4. Paste it into section 7 of the Studio — it will flow into the program automatically, and the text sizes itself to fit.
